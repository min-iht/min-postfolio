Beyond The CV
: A Backstory

When I was little, my mother asked a fortune teller, “What will this child grow up to be?”
The fortune teller said, “She is fated to roam foreign lands as if they were her own backyard.”
Hearing this, my mother was sure I would become a diplomat.
Instead, the moment I came of age, I left on a solo backpacking trip funded by a lifetime of New Year's money.
In university, I all but lived in the library, winning a scholarship every semester so I could travel every vacation.
At thirty, I kept my promise to mark the year with an open-ended journey.
My mother, who had long since learned that nagging was useless on me, quietly worried —
until I came home from a long journey saying I had found what I wanted to do, reborn as a morning person for the first time in twelve years — and she finally relaxed.
Then the fortune teller's words came back to me: “The inspiration she gathers from her travels will make her a great figure.” (In Korean, “inspiration” can also mean “old man” — surely she meant the first one?)
These days I am no longer the child my mother aches over, but a talent with promise — looking for ground to put down roots.
